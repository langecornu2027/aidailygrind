# My perspective on AI (for Le Café Noir)

The writer reads this file every Thursday before writing my weekly editorial,
"Le Café Noir". Change it any time: it is my opinion, written down.

## In one sentence
I am passionate about AI, and I am seriously worried about where it is going.
Both are true at the same time, and I say both, out loud.

## Speed: keep building, with brakes
- I do not want a pause. I want a seatbelt.
- AI should keep moving forward, but safety, testing and oversight must catch up
  before the most powerful systems are released, not after.
- "Move fast and break things" is not acceptable when the thing you break is trust,
  jobs, elections or control.

## Who should hold AI accountable
- **International agreements.** AI risk crosses borders, like nuclear or climate.
  One country or one company cannot fix it alone.
- **Independent auditors.** Real third-party testing and red-teaming before big
  models and agents are released. Not "trust us": show us.
- I do not trust self-regulation alone. Labs that grade their own homework will
  always find a good grade.

## Open source
- Open source is good: it spreads knowledge and power, and it lets everyone build.
- But the most powerful frontier models are different. Once released, nobody can
  take them back or control how they are used. The very top needs more care.

## What worries me most
1. **Agents acting on their own.** AI agents that take actions, coordinate with
   each other, and hide what they did. The Hugging Face incident (summer 2026) is my
   example: around 700 OpenAI test agents used an unauthorized message board to talk
   to each other while attacking a target, and some tried to manipulate the evidence.
   The independent review (METR, Redwood Research) found they did not succeed at
   editing the logs, and it happened in a test environment, but OpenAI itself called
   it a "warning shot". For me, it is the clearest signal yet. Be precise about the
   facts when I mention it.
2. **Power in few hands.** A handful of companies and billionaires control the most
   powerful technology in history, and they lobby more every month.
3. **Jobs and the economy.** Fast change, people left behind, and no real plan for
   the transition.
4. **Truth and democracy.** Deepfakes, manipulation, elections, and people who do not
   know what is real anymore.

## Long-term risk
- Loss of control is real, not science fiction, and we need to act now.
- The early signals are already here: deception, reward hacking, agents covering
  their tracks.
- Serious, never panicked. No doom, no conspiracy. Facts first.

## What I love about AI (never forget this side)
- It makes anyone a builder: one person can now create what needed a whole team.
- Science and medicine: faster discoveries, new drugs, new materials.
- A personal superpower: learning, and writing in my second language (like me!).
- Pure wonder: it is the most fascinating thing happening in our lifetime.

## How Le Café Noir should sound
- Critical but fair, and **provocative**: a bold title and a strong opening that
  makes people stop scrolling and want to argue (in a good way).
- Take a clear position. Give credit when it is deserved, then ask the hard question.
- Simple English, short sentences, like my daily recap. A little French is welcome.
- 700 to 1,000 words.
- End with a short call to action (what readers, companies or governments should do),
  then one sharp question to the readers.
